<TldrBlock>
A new build on Isla Verde costs about 22 percent more per square metre than a resale of the same size in the same district, at the Isla Verde Land Registry's first-half 2026 medians. It buys a warranty, low early service charges and a modern plan; the resale buys the accounts, the actual charge and a price with the developer's margin removed.
</TldrBlock>

## Side by side

| Criterion | New build, completed | Resale, 5 to 15 years old |
|---|---|---|
| Median price premium, H1 2026 | plus 22 percent | baseline |
| Structural warranty | 5 years from handover | remainder, if under 5 years |
| Service charge, first two years | about a third lower | full rate |
| Association accounts to read | none yet | three years or more |
| Reserve fund | starts at zero | should hold 10 percent of budget |
| Delay risk before keys | none once completed | none |
| Choice of units | what the developer has left | what is listed |

## Where the new build wins

Certainty about the building's condition and the cost of the first years. Nothing has failed, the warranty covers what does, and the association charge is low because the reserve fund has not been called on. A buyer who wants to move in and think about nothing for five years is buying that.

The premium is the price of the certainty, and it is real: a 55 sqm one-bedroom at the new-build median [on the marina in Playa Norte](/areas/playa-norte/) costs about 29,000 CRC more than its resale equivalent. The [off-plan guide](/guides/off-plan-versus-completed-what-changes/) explains how buying before completion claws some of that back, at a different risk.

## Where the resale wins

Information and price. Three years of association accounts tell you what the building actually costs to run and whether the roof is paid for, which no brochure can. The price has had the developer's margin taken out by the first owner, and a resale in a building under five years old still carries the warranty.

<Callout kind="tip">
The best value on the island in 2026 is the four-year-old resale: warranty still running, accounts available, first owner's premium gone.
</Callout>

## Pros and cons of each

The new build's pros are condition, warranty and low early charges; its cons are the premium, an association with no history and a reserve fund at zero. The resale's pros are the accounts, the price and a settled building; its cons are wear, a service charge at the full rate, and the chance of a special assessment the minutes will reveal if you read them.

## Risks and what to check

For a new build, check that the developer has registered the association and handed over the reserve fund, because some hold it for a year. For a resale, read the minutes for voted assessments and the reserve balance, as the [service charges guide](/guides/service-charges-in-isla-verde-condo-buildings/) shows. Both need the [title checks](/guides/title-checks-before-you-pay-a-deposit/), and the permit check matters most for the resale in a converted building.

## Who should pick which: scenarios

The buyer who values a quiet first five years and can pay the premium buys new. The buyer who reads accounts and wants the price buys the four-year-old resale. The buyer with time and no need to borrow reads the [off-plan guide](/guides/off-plan-versus-completed-what-changes/) and considers a launch. In every case the [condo buying guide](/guides/buying-a-condo-on-isla-verde/) gives the order of steps from deposit to keys.
