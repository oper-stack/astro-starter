<TldrBlock>
Playa Norte for visitor income and resale speed, Cerro Alto for living on the island year round. The same 132,000 CRC buys a 55 sqm one-bedroom on the marina or a 120 sqm three-bedroom on the ridge, at the Isla Verde Land Registry's first-half 2026 medians, and the choice is about how you will use it, not about which is the better district.
</TldrBlock>

## Side by side

| Criterion | Playa Norte | Cerro Alto |
|---|---|---|
| Median condo price per sqm, H1 2026 | 2,400 CRC | 1,100 CRC |
| Service charge per sqm a month | 2.8 to 3.2 CRC | 1.8 to 2.0 CRC |
| Year-round occupation of units | 31 percent | 88 percent |
| Typical tenant | visitor, 3 to 14 nights | family, 12 month lease |
| Median days on market, 2025 | 41 | 67 |
| Distance to school | 15 minutes | 12 minutes |
| Distance to marina and ferry | on foot | 25 minutes by road |
| Title | condominium, perpetual | condominium or freehold plot |

## Where Playa Norte wins

Income from visitors and the speed of resale. The marina district books the winter months that carry a short-let unit's year, and its units sold in a median 41 days in 2025. A buyer who will use the unit for a few weeks and let it the rest of the time has no real alternative on the island; the [Playa Norte profile](/areas/playa-norte/) has the building-by-building detail.

## Where Cerro Alto wins

Space, title and the cost of owning. The ridge sells three bedrooms for the price of one, every plot is freehold, and the service charges are the lowest on Isla Verde because the buildings have no pools and few lifts. A family gets the school run and the view, and the [Cerro Alto profile](/areas/cerro-alto/) shows what the budget buys.

## Pros and cons of each

The marina's pros are demand and liquidity; its cons are the price, the service charges the salt air drives, and a summer quiet enough to make a short-let owner nervous. The ridge's pros are space, freehold title and low running costs; its cons are the road, the absence of visitor income and a slower sale when the time comes.

## Risks and what to check

On the marina, check the building's minimum stay rule and its reserve fund before buying for short lets. On the ridge, check the permit against the house if it stands on its own plot. Both districts need the [title checks](/guides/title-checks-before-you-pay-a-deposit/) in full, and both carry the completion costs the [fees guide](/guides/fees-and-taxes-at-completion/) sets out, which are the same percentage in either.

## Who should pick which: scenarios

The buyer who will live on the island most of the year, with or without children, belongs on the ridge. The buyer who wants a winter base and a rental the rest of the year belongs on the marina. The buyer who cannot decide should read the [condo buying guide](/guides/buying-a-condo-on-isla-verde/) and then visit both on a weekday in August, when the marina is empty and the ridge is not.
