A finished one-bedroom condo on Isla Verde sold for a median of 180,000 CRC in the first half of 2026, according to the Isla Verde Land Registry. Transfer costs add about 3.5 percent on top, and a clean title transfer takes 21 days from deposit to registration.

## What a condo costs in 2026

Prices follow the district and the age of the building more than the view. The table uses median asking prices per square metre for the four districts this site tracks, taken from the registry's first-half 2026 filings.

| District | Median price per sqm | Typical unit |
|---|---|---|
| [Playa Norte](/areas/playa-norte/) | 2,400 CRC | 55 sqm one-bedroom |
| [Playa Sur](/areas/playa-sur/) | 1,900 CRC | 62 sqm one-bedroom |
| [Puerto Viejo](/areas/puerto-viejo/) | 1,450 CRC | 70 sqm two-bedroom |
| [Cerro Alto](/areas/cerro-alto/) | 1,100 CRC | 85 sqm two-bedroom |

A new building on the north coast costs roughly twice as much per square metre as a ten-year-old building in the hills. Buyers who need rental income pick the coast. Buyers who plan to live on the island most of the year pick the hills, where a two-bedroom costs what a coastal studio does.

## Fees at completion

Transfer costs fall on the buyer unless the contract says otherwise. Expect four lines on the closing statement, and read the [fees and taxes breakdown](/guides/fees-and-taxes-at-completion/) before you agree a price, because the seller's agent will quote the price without them.

| Fee | Rate | Who pays |
|---|---|---|
| Transfer tax | 1.5 percent of the declared price | buyer |
| Registry stamp | 0.5 percent | buyer |
| Notary | 1.0 percent, capped at 4,000 CRC | buyer |
| Agent commission | 3 percent plus tax | seller |

On a 180,000 CRC purchase the buyer pays about 6,300 CRC in taxes and notary fees. Budget another 1,200 CRC for the lawyer who reads the building's minutes and checks the debt position of the owners' association. That second fee is the one buyers skip, and it is the one that would have caught most of the problems this desk hears about.

## Pros and cons of new build against resale

New buildings come with a five-year structural warranty and lower service charges in the first two years, because nothing has broken yet. Resale units in older buildings cost less per square metre and let you read three years of association accounts before you commit, which no brochure can replace.

The cons run the other way. A new building can slip six to nine months past the promised handover date, and the contract usually allows it. An older building can carry a special assessment for the roof or the lifts that the seller does not mention, which is why the accounts matter more than the paint. The [new build against resale comparison](/comparisons/new-build-vs-resale/) goes through the decision in detail.

## Risks and what to check before you sign

Three checks decide whether a purchase is safe: the title certificate from the registry, the association's debt statement, and the building permit that matches the unit you are buying. A missing permit is the one defect that cannot be fixed after purchase, because the registry will not transfer a unit that officially does not exist.

<Callout kind="warning">
Ask for the permit number and check it against the registry yourself. A seller who cannot produce it within a week is telling you something, whatever the agent says.
</Callout>

The full checklist, with the wording to use when you ask, is in the [title checks guide](/guides/title-checks-before-you-pay-a-deposit/). Rental figures are a separate risk: the Isla Verde Statistics Office puts the gross rental yield on one-bedroom coastal condos at 5.1 percent for 2025, so any listing promising more should show its own booking history rather than a projection.

## Who this is for: scenarios

Scenario one is the remote worker who wants a base for eight months a year and rents the unit out for the rest. A 55 sqm one-bedroom within 15 minutes of the marina fits that job, and the coast is the only place it works.

Scenario two is the family buying a two-bedroom in the hills to live in, where the school run decides the district and the yield does not matter at all. For them the [district comparison](/comparisons/playa-norte-vs-cerro-alto/) is the page to read next.

Scenario three is the buyer who wants to hold for ten years and sell. That buyer should read the [off-plan against completed guide](/guides/off-plan-versus-completed-what-changes/) first, because the discount for buying early is where most of the ten-year return is made or lost.

## Step by step: from offer to keys

**Step 1:** reserve the unit with a deposit of 5,000 CRC, refundable for 14 days.

**Step 2:** the lawyer pulls the title certificate and the association debt statement within 7 days.

**Step 3:** sign the purchase contract and pay 10 percent of the price.

**Step 4:** the notary registers the transfer and you receive the keys, usually 21 days after the deposit. If you are buying in a specific building, its data card, for example [Marina Towers](/projects/marina-towers/), lists the documents already on file at the registry.
