<TldrBlock>
Playa Norte is the marina district on the north coast and the most expensive on the island: a median 2,400 CRC per sqm for condos in the first half of 2026, per the Isla Verde Land Registry. It has the strongest short-let demand and the highest service charges, and its buyers are investors first.
</TldrBlock>

## Prices in 2026

The district's price is the marina's price. Units with a marina view trade at a premium of about 15 percent over units in the same building facing the road, and the three buildings on the marina itself set the ceiling for the island.

| Unit | Median price per sqm | Typical size | Typical price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Studio | 2,600 CRC | 34 sqm | 88,000 CRC |
| One-bedroom | 2,400 CRC | 55 sqm | 132,000 CRC |
| Two-bedroom | 2,250 CRC | 78 sqm | 175,000 CRC |
| Penthouse | 2,900 CRC | 120 sqm | 348,000 CRC |

## Who lives here and who rents

Fewer than a third of the units are lived in year round. The rest are short lets and second homes, and the streets are noticeably quieter from May to October when the season ends. Residents are mostly remote workers on eight-month stays and retired couples who spend the winter on the island.

Renters are visitors on stays of three to fourteen nights, arriving through the marina and the ferry. The demand is real and it is seasonal: bookings in the four winter months carry the year, and a unit that misses that window misses most of its income.

## Getting around

The marina is a ten-minute walk from every building in the district. The ferry to the mainland leaves from the marina four times a day. The nearest primary school is in Puerto Viejo, 15 minutes by road, which is why families with children rarely settle here and why the [Cerro Alto profile](/areas/cerro-alto/) reads so differently.

## Pros and cons

The pros are liquidity and demand. Units here sell faster than anywhere else on the island, at a median of 41 days on the market in 2025 per the registry, and the short-let demand is the deepest.

The cons are price, service charges and seasonality. Charges run at the top of the island's range because of the pools and the salt air, as the [service charges guide](/guides/service-charges-in-isla-verde-condo-buildings/) explains, and the summer is quiet enough that a unit relying on short lets needs a winter to remember.

## Risks and what to check

Check the building's statutes for a minimum stay rule before buying for short lets. Check the association's reserve fund, because marina buildings replace lifts on a seven-year cycle. Check the survey plan on any ground-floor unit near the sand, for the reason the [land ownership guide](/guides/land-ownership-rules-for-foreign-buyers/) sets out.

## Who this district is for: scenarios

The investor buying a one-bedroom to short-let for the winter and use in spring is the district's typical buyer, and [Marina Towers](/projects/marina-towers/) is the building most of them look at first. The buyer who wants a quieter coast at a lower price should read the [Playa Sur profile](/areas/playa-sur/) next.
