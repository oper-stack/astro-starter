<TldrBlock>
Puerto Viejo is the port town and the island's only year-round economy: condos at a median 1,450 CRC per sqm in the first half of 2026, per the Isla Verde Land Registry, tenants who stay, and the school and the hospital that the coastal districts lack. Buyers here are residents and long-let landlords, not short-let investors.
</TldrBlock>

## Prices in 2026

The town's prices are set by wages, not by visitors. The converted warehouses along the old quay are the exception, where lofts of 90 to 140 sqm trade to buyers from the mainland at a premium over the rest of the town.

| Unit | Median price per sqm | Typical size | Typical price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Two-bedroom condo, town | 1,450 CRC | 70 sqm | 101,500 CRC |
| Three-bedroom condo, town | 1,350 CRC | 95 sqm | 128,000 CRC |
| Warehouse loft, quay | 1,950 CRC | 110 sqm | 214,500 CRC |
| Town house, freehold | 1,200 CRC | 130 sqm | 156,000 CRC |

## Who lives here

Almost everyone, all year. The port employs about 1,200 people, the hospital 400 and the school 90, and the town's units are lived in by them and rented to them. The registry recorded 92 percent year-round occupation in the town in its 2025 census of units, against 31 percent on the north coast.

Tenants sign twelve-month leases and renew. A landlord here collects rent in August as well as in January, which no coastal district can promise.

## The school, the hospital and the ferry

The island's only primary and secondary schools are in the town, which is why families from every district drive here twice a day, and why the [Cerro Alto profile](/areas/cerro-alto/) describes a 12 minute school run as its main selling point. The hospital is on the ring road, and the mainland ferry from [Playa Norte](/areas/playa-norte/) is a 25 minute drive.

## Pros and cons

The pros are occupancy, tenants who stay and the lowest service charges on the island, because the town buildings have no pools and few lifts. The warehouse lofts add a kind of unit no other district has.

The cons are that there is no beach and no visitor demand, and that prices move with the port's fortunes rather than with tourism. A buyer who wants capital growth from visitor demand is in the wrong district.

## Risks and what to check

The warehouse conversions need the permit check more than any other units on the island, because two of the five conversions were split into more units than the permit allows. The [title checks guide](/guides/title-checks-before-you-pay-a-deposit/) explains what to compare on the permit. Town buildings older than twenty years need the reserve fund check from the [service charges guide](/guides/service-charges-in-isla-verde-condo-buildings/).

## Who this district is for: scenarios

The buyer who works on the island, the landlord who wants one tenant on a twelve-month lease, and the mainland buyer who wants a quay loft with a freehold title all fit here. [Puerto Lofts](/projects/puerto-lofts/) is the conversion with the cleanest paperwork on file. The visitor-income buyer should read the [condo buying guide](/guides/buying-a-condo-on-isla-verde/) and look to the coasts.
